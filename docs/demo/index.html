What the drone does

  1. Find gates in the image. Threshold the orange paint, fit a four-cornered outline to each gate and to its opening (outlined in the camera view).
  2. Measure where the gate is. A gate's known size and its four corners give its position relative to the camera (perspective-n-point).
  3. Fuse. An error-state Kalman filter tracks position, velocity and attitude in the frame of the gate it is flying to, so no map is needed. Bad measurements are rejected with a chi-squared test.
  4. Hand off. Once past a gate, the state is re-expressed in the next gate's frame, estimated from sightings made on the approach.
  5. Fly. Guidance aims through the gate centre at race pace and is turned into thrust and body-rate commands, like an FPV pilot in acro mode.

Camera only drops the IMU. Between camera frames the filter predicts motion from the drone's own commands through a simple vehicle model.
